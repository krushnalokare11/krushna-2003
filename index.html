01 About

I am currently studying Mathematical Sciences (Integrated BS-MS) at IISER Thiruvananthapuram, and my research interests include analysis of nonlinear PDEs and phase-field modelling.

My current MSc thesis work under Dr. Sheetal Dharmatti focuses on well-posedness, regularity, and long-time behaviour of solutions of (nonlocal) Cahn–Hilliard type systems, with emphasis on nonlocal Cahn–Hilliard equations driven by singular potentials, analysis of (nonlocal) Cahn–Hilliard–Navier–Stokes systems, and logarithmic free energies, utilising primarily energy methods, Sobolev embeddings, and compactness and fixed-point arguments.

Some previous research activities include investigation of Physics-Informed Neural Networks for nonlinear and higher-order PDEs, finite-volume ENO and Neural-ENO methods for discontinuities, approximation of ENO/ENO-SR schemes using ReLU networks and construction of a stencil-shift classification network (DeLENO), and statistical inference and simulation studies related to reliability and renewal processes.

I am interested in questions related to computational PDEs, phase-field modelling, cross-diffusion systems, and scientific machine learning, and I am looking forward to pursuing a PhD in Applied Mathematics.

03 Research projects

Coursework and computational research work most relevant to my current interests.

Finite element methods

Nonlocal FEM–FETI Solver for Nonlocal Diffusion Problems

This project focused on solving nonlocal diffusion problems using a finite element and FETI-based domain decomposition framework. The work considered nonlocal interactions through a horizon parameter, built overlapping subdomains, and implemented the projected PCG solver to compute the numerical solution efficiently.

Methods Finite element method, domain decomposition, FETI, projected PCG, nonlocal diffusion

Tools MATLAB, LaTeX, numerical linear algebra

Code Report Presentation Reference paper

Artificial intelligence · Search algorithms

Comparative Analysis of Breadth-First and A* Search Algorithms for the Eight-Puzzle Problem

A course project examining BFS and A* search for solving the 8-puzzle problem. I implemented both search algorithms, designed heuristic functions including Manhattan Distance for informed search, and analyzed performance using time, memory, node expansions, and path length. I also built interactive web-based simulations to visualize search paths and algorithm behaviour, strengthening my skills in algorithm design, computational analysis, testing, and visualization.

Methods Breadth-First Search, A* search, Manhattan Distance, state-space search
